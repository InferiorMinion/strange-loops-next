Trust: who counts, walked

A line proves which key signed it. Trust is everything between that and "this record counts". Walked end to end, it comes apart into five strata, each reading only the ones below. Two tiers of key are enough. And retire through turns out to need a tick, so standing splits in two around step 4.

proposal Nothing here is settled or built. The first product slice writes only as its signing key, and one draft model exists for stratum 1. No anchor that anyone means to keep should be made until this walk is closed.

Why strata. The outside review's finding D: admission reads grants, grants are admitted by standing, and standing is a fold over key bindings signed by keys whose standing comes from an anchor. That is a loop unless each question reads only answers already fixed. Stratifying by kind makes it a ladder.

The walk

One store, three writers: you, an agent seat working with you, and a second person who joins and later leaves. Each step says whether the design so far holds or breaks.

  1. You start. One record of kind lineage-anchor, whose observer is the key that signed it. Its hash is your sl: id, and that key is your root.

    Holds, with one decision inside it: the root needs nothing from the payload, so erasing or withholding the anchor's payload never changes who you are. Your name and email are separate records about the lineage.

  2. You write from the workbench. A key-binding record, observer your lineage, about the workbench's device key. Records the device key signs with observer: sl:you are now your word.

    Breaks the four-tier key scheme, in a useful way. Who may sign a binding? If any bound key may, a stolen device key can bind more keys, and two keys can each try to end the other. If only the root may, stratum 1 has no recursion at all: the speakers are the root, plus what the root bound.

    So: two tiers. The root signs every record that changes standing. Device keys sign everything else. "Presence" stops being a third key and becomes what the root is: the key that asks for you before it signs. This is the review's "two key tiers, not four".

  3. An agent works with you. It is not a key of yours. The seat has its own lineage and its own root, and your root signs a grant naming it, with a scope. Its records carry under, pointing at the grant.

    Holds. The agent's key is worth little because the grant, not the key, is what makes its records count, and you end the grant without touching its key. Session keys can come later as device keys of the seat; nothing needs them yet.

    The honest limit stays: an agent running as your OS user can read a device key kept in a file, and sign ordinary records as you. It can't change standing unless it can also reach the root. Where your root lives is the real decision, below.

  4. The store gets a steward. A store-anchor names the lineages that may change the store's standing. Solo, that is you.

    Breaks the earlier sketch, which kept stewards in the payload. A payload can be erased or withheld, and a store whose stewards can be erased has no stewards. Either the standing kinds keep everything standing reads in the envelope, or the standing kinds can't be erased. The first is one rule instead of two, and it is what the anchor and the binding already do.

  5. A second person joins. A steward's policy record adds their lineage as a writer. Their anchor and bindings are copied in, so the store verifies itself.

    Holds, and shows what admission is: a record counts when its key speaks for its observer (stratum 1) and the store's policy takes that observer (stratum 3). The first is not negotiable at any maturity, or anyone could write as anyone. Only the second varies from open to strict.

  6. Everything so far only grows. No rule yet takes standing away. More records never silence a key, unbind a writer or end a grant, and arrival order can't matter. The laws already proved for folds and for late arrivals carry over unchanged.

    Holds. This half of standing is small, and the draft model proves its stratum 1.

  7. The second person leaves, and a device is lost. Both are "retire through": a cut by what was held, never by time, since emitted can be backdated.

    Breaks on what "through" means. The Sharing walk leaned on a segment being one writer's growing file, so "through r4" names a prefix. A store is a set. The first product slice closes a segment per changeset, export | import regroups lines, and an erasure rewrites a segment. There is no position to cut at that every replica agrees on.

    What every replica can agree on is a set of ids. A retirement has to cover the records it lets stand, and "a signed record that covers a set of ids" is a witness tick. The Sharing tab already said so: "a witness tick in miniature".

  8. So retiring waits for ticks. It needs the tick's way of covering a set compactly, and the result encoding that question D7 leaves open.

    This settles the order the review argued about. Standing is not one step. The growing half (anchors, bindings, stewards, grants, admission) can come now. Ticks come next. Retiring comes after, as a tick with a stance.

  9. A retirement arrives late. A replica that already counted the lost device's records must stop counting the ones outside the cover.

    Holds, as a known shape: it is the same fact as a late prev target retracting an admission. The set still decides; a cached state can't follow it and must be rebuilt for that key's records. The review's law A fits here: the cut ends a key's standing after it and nothing before it, and grants the key signed before the cut stand.

Where it strains. A grant's scope (which stores, which kinds) does not fit an envelope. If the scope is payload, a reader without that payload can't judge a record written under the grant. Admission would then answer three ways, as where does: yes, no, or unknown, with the unknowns counted beside the result. That is open, below.

A side effect. A record about a lineage joins that lineage's thing. A store anchor about its stewards shows up in each steward's state. That may be right (it is something true about them) or noise. Also open.

What the draft model already proves, and what it gets wrong

model/Loops/Keys.lean on the branch step3/key-binding models stratum 1 without retirement. It proves that holding more never silences a key, that the set decides, that withholding payloads changes nothing, that there is no speaker without an anchor, and that a stranger's binding binds nothing.

The walk overrules one thing in it: the draft lets any speaking key sign a binding. With two tiers only the root does, and the rule gets shorter. The anchor's shape survives the walk unchanged.

Decisions this needs from you

Where does your root key live?

The root signs every standing change, so it is the one key worth protecting. On the workbench it would be a file any process running as you can use, agents included. On a laptop keychain or a hardware key it asks for you, and binding the workbench's device key becomes one signed record carried over.

leaning Not on the workbench. Until a second machine runs loops, nothing canonical gets anchored there.

Two tiers, with the root as presence?

It costs one thing: adding a device always needs the root, where the four-tier design let an existing device vouch. It buys a stratum 1 with no recursion and no question of which key may end which.

leaning Yes.

Standing reads envelopes only?

The alternative is a second rule: standing kinds can't be erased or withheld. Envelope-only needs no exception, but pushes steward and subject lists into about, and leaves a grant's scope with nowhere to go.

leaning Envelope-only for who; payload for scope, with three-way admission.

Split standing around ticks?

3a (anchors, bindings, stewards, grants, admission), then ticks, then 3b (retiring). Until 3b exists, a lost key or a departed writer can only be handled by starting a new lineage or a new store, which is honest for a solo store and not for a team.

leaning Yes. It also means D7, the result encoding, gates retiring as well as ticks.

What this walk didn't touch

  • Forked policy. Two stewards change policy apart. The review's answer: the newest head by content order is in effect, the fork is shown. Not walked.
  • Declarations and maturity. Which kinds and shapes a strict store takes, judged at emitted. That is the other half of step 3 and reads nothing from this half but who the stewards are.
  • Names. Name tables and @home resolve at read and never take part in matching.
  • Across realms. Vouches, follows and federation's admission.
  • Receipt evidence. A backdated record from a stolen key, before anyone retires it. A covering tick is the evidence; this walk only shows where it plugs in.
  • Proving presence to other replicas, and a signer behind a socket. Both stay open under Sharing.
